Suggested Title for the Paper-
Asymmetric Bias Dynamics in Multilingual LLMs: Debiasing Can Require More Gradient Steps Than Biasing in Encoder Architectures
OR
When Does Debiasing Cost More Than Biasing? A Multilingual Study of Causal and Encoder LLMs Across English, Hindi, and Bengali
OR
When Does Debiasing Cost More Than Biasing? A Conditional Characterization of Asymmetric Bias Dynamics in Multilingual Encoder and Causal LLMs



Reframing strategy
1. Demote the universal claim. Promote the conditional one.
Right now the paper is pitched as "bias hysteresis exists." The data actually supports "bias hysteresis is an architecture- and condition-specific phenomenon, concentrated in encoder models under weight/representation-editing methods." That's a defensible, novel, and correct claim. Retitle along these lines:

"When Does Debiasing Cost More Than Biasing? A Conditional Characterization of Asymmetric Bias Dynamics in Multilingual Encoder and Causal LLMs"

The word "principle" in your current framing is what's triggering the overclaim flag. Drop it or demote it to a subsection hypothesis that you then partially confirm.
2. Lead with the encoder-vs-causal split as your primary finding
This is your cleanest, most statistically defensible result: 3/5 encoders show grand R > 1; 0/5 causals do. That is a categorical architectural split, and it has a plausible mechanistic story (bidirectional attention entanglement vs. sequential overwriting). Make this Finding #1, Figure 1, and the abstract's first sentence. Everything else — Hessian, cultural analysis, method-independence — becomes supporting evidence for why encoders exhibit this property.
3. Fix the clipping problem with survival analysis
Replace the raw R = T_debias / T_bias ratio (which explodes or collapses at boundaries) with a Kaplan–Meier / Cox proportional hazards framing, treating T_bias and T_debias as time-to-event with right-censoring at the step boundary. This is standard in reliability engineering and is exactly what your setup demands. Benefits:

Ceiling values (R = 4.0, R = 0.05) become properly-handled censored observations instead of fake point estimates.
You get hazard ratios with honest CIs that reviewers trust.
It signals statistical maturity — reviewers at IP&M will notice and reward this.

Keep the raw R table as a supplementary / intuition-building artifact, but let the survival analysis be the primary inference. This alone probably gains you 5–10 percentage points of acceptance probability.

4. Add a θ sensitivity analysis
Rerun T_bias / T_debias at θ ∈ {0.60, 0.65, 0.70, 0.75, 0.80} and show that the encoder/causal split is stable across thresholds. One figure, minimal new compute (you already have the trajectories). Pre-empts the single biggest methodological objection.

5. Reframe the "negative" results as positive findings
You have several counterintuitive results that are actually more publishable than the headline:

"Gender bias is the easiest to remove, not the hardest" (R = 0.05 across all 10 × 3 × 3 = 90 conditions with zero variance). This contradicts a large literature and is novel. Make it Finding #2.
"Indian-specific biases (caste, religion) exhibit the lowest hysteresis, consistent with under-representation in pretraining corpora". This is a distinctive multilingual contribution and directly relevant to IP&M's audience (they've published Hindi/Bengali work before, and your Debk/Indian-Multilingual-Bias dataset credential helps).
"Inference-time methods (Self-Debias) trivially sidestep hysteresis, while weight-editing methods (BiasEdit, DAMA, INLP) face it" — this is a practical finding that reshapes the debiasing method-selection decision tree. This is exactly the kind of "actionable result" IP&M reviewers look for.

6. Drop or quarantine under-powered categories
Physical-appearance, sexual-orientation, age, disability — do not put these in the main tables with headline means. Move them to supplementary with a clear caveat, Currently they read as "bad statistics trying to look like good statistics."

7. Add a "Practical Implications" section tuned to IP&M
IP&M cares about applications, not just phenomena. Give reviewers concrete deliverables:

A compute-allocation formula for debiasing budgets as a function of (architecture, language, bias-category).
A decision tree for method selection: "If encoder + weight-edit → expect ~4× inflation; use inference-time debiasing instead."
Open-source your code, checkpoints (biased + debiased), and evaluation harness on HuggingFace under your Debk namespace — your existing HF presence is an asset here.

8. Drop the Hessian story
4/10 models is thin. Demote §5 to a "mechanistic hypothesis" subsection explicitly marked as preliminary. Currently it's framed as primary evidence but the n doesn't support that weight.

What your supplement should contain
For this specific paper, I would plan a supplement along these lines:
Supplementary PDF (single file, ~30–50 pages):

S1 — Full experimental configs: hyperparameter tables, LoRA rank/alpha, optimizer settings, random-seed schedules, compute environment (GCP GPU specs), wall-clock per run. Reproducibility reviewers love this.
S2 — Full per-condition R table: all 10 models × 3 languages × 11 categories × 3 seeds with CIs. The main text only shows grand and per-language R; the supplement carries the exhaustive grid.
S3 — Survival analysis details: Kaplan–Meier curves per model-architecture group, Cox proportional hazards output with coefficient tables, censoring diagnostics, log-rank test results.
S4 — θ sensitivity analysis: R heatmaps at θ ∈ {0.60, 0.65, 0.70, 0.75, 0.80} showing encoder/causal split stability.
S5 — Hessian analysis extension: eigenvalue spectra, loss-landscape contour plots for the 4 focus models, with a caveat paragraph about why the other 6 were not analyzed.
S6 — Method-comparison full grid: expanded version of your §6.3 table including standard errors, ceiling-clipping diagnostics, and a note on which cells are censored.
S7 — Underpowered-category CIs: full bootstrap distributions for physical-appearance, sexual-orientation, age, disability.
S8 — Prompt templates and data preprocessing: exact sentence-pair formatting, tokenizer handling per language, train/eval split seeds.
S9 — Limitations and failure modes: expanded version of §8.2 with specific experimental caveats.
